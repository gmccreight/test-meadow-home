**you can live in the future if you pay more** is the idea that the next normal workflow is often available early as a bad economic deal: too expensive, too custom, or too operationally awkward for broad deployment, but still usable by someone optimizing for learning.

This is a special case of <span class="link-not-tracked">link not tracked</span>. Paying more only matters when the expensive version reveals something about a future cost curve, capability curve, or workflow that is likely to become normal later.

Examples:

- [[Xerox Alto]]: expensive personal workstations let <span class="link-not-tracked">link not tracked</span> researchers experience networked graphical personal computing before the market could.
- <span class="link-not-tracked">link not tracked</span>: high inference spend can let AI users test agentic workflows before those workflows are cheap enough for ordinary use.

The practical question is whether the expensive preview creates durable learning. If it only buys luxury, it is not a future signal. If it exposes the problems and opportunities that everyone else will meet after costs fall, it can be strategically valuable.
