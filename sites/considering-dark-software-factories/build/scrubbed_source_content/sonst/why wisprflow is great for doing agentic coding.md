^ why [[project - wispr flow|wisprflow]] is great for doing [[agentic coding]]

---

Modern [[AI unit]]s like [[AI unit - claude 4.6 Opus|claude 4.6 Opus]] can easily understand your intent even if the _exact words_ aren't perfectly captured.  If instead of carefully typing out your prompt, if you provide 50% more context because you're just fluidly talking, you'll often end up with a better result.
...
[[quickly providing much more context is often more useful than providing less context more carefully]]

[[project - wispr flow|Wisprflow]], unlike the [[Mac's built in speech-to-text]], isn't tied to streaming words into a specific input area.  This frees you up to navigate around your full system while recording yourself talking, which allows you to do things like:

- Read through your specs and simply mention specific sections that seem pertinent to what you're asking it to do
- Browse around in the code in your editor and just mention the files that seem applicable, patterns you wish it would adopt in places
- Actually QA your app and describe bugs you encounter in real time

Essentially, you just sort of vibe with an extemporaneous spoken chain of thought, and at the end, you go back to your coding agent, press the finish button, and it writes everything into the coding agent's input area.  Then your coding agent takes over.

In practice, this works very well.   It is often superior to (and significantly easier than) carefully hand-crafting prompts.
