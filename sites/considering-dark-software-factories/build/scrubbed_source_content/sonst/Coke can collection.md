When I was a kid, I had a Coke can collection. Early on, the [[collector|collecting]] logic felt obvious: save the cans that marked some clearly different Coca-Cola object in the world.

New Coke had been introduced, so that was obviously worth having a can for. Then Coca-Cola reintroduced Coke Classic, which also deserved a slot. Cherry Coke felt important at the time because I loved it, so that went in too.

The international cans were even better. When Dad traveled to Europe on Lufthansa, he brought back a mini can of Coke from the flight, and the size alone blew my mind. In Europe, Diet Coke was called Coke Light, which also seemed incredible: same product family, different country, different name, clearly worth adding.

As the collection got more mature, the <span class="link-not-tracked">link not tracked</span> got harder to maintain. A Great America promotional can had some mildly interesting graphics, but was that a real member of the collection, or just an ad printed on an ordinary Coke can? Slightly larger or smaller cans raised the same question.

At first I was collecting obvious product events. Eventually I was deciding whether surface variation, promotion, geography, and package size were enough to make a can count.

Maybe that is one reason collections are interesting: they start as delight and turn into a small <span class="link-not-tracked">link not tracked</span> problem.
