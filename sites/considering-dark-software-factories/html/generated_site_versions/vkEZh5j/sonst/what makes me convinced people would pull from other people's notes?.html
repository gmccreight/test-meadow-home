what makes me convinced people would pull from other people's notes?

The Problem

You really want your notes to be a reflection of your current and evolving understanding of things. Once you start incorporating content from sources other than your own thinking, the question becomes "do you really understand or really own your own notes?" To take an obviously overblown example, if you copied an entire Wikipedia page into your notes, do you still maintain cognitive ownership over all the content in that note?

If you use the process of writing to think, what does it mean when the stuff that is written in your notes was written by others... be it AI, or notes that you're grabbing from people?

Thoughts

It really depends on the length of the content

You can assimilate a sentence or even a paragraph. Once it gets longer, you'd have to really work to fully assimilate all the ideas if you didn't write them.

We are already using AI-generated content in notes

With link not tracked tooling like using company - Cursor for link not tracked and link not tracked, AI is already writing into our notes. It fills things in here and there, but tends to give bounded answers.

We can annotate notes written by others

In this chat note link not tracked, which is a kind of chat note written inside Obsidian by an LLM chat plugin, you can see that I am trying to assimilate the ideas by asking followup questions, then linking the responses to existing notes, and even annotating the final paragraph with some related concepts.

We are used to working with others on concepts

Anybody who has working on a large codebase has the experience of having shared ownership over a large artifact. Out of necessity they must develop and use a process for reviewing and assimilating externally created changes into their own mental model of how the system works. You might be the original author of sections of the overall codebase, but over time, other authors will modify your original approach, making it less recognizable. You can watch the changes come in, and modify your mental model along the way.

The notes are bounded and cohesive

link not tracked ... so it should be very easy to decide which concepts in the other person's notes you want to pull locally. Those concepts are like functions in a well-factored functional codebase that you can pull generally useful functions from. Since they are bounded and cohesive, they can easily be recontextualized. Tooling could help a lot here.
